Environment

Light provides energy for photosynthesis. Effect saturates at high intensity rather than growing without limit.
Activity peaks near an optimum temperature (~25°C) and falls off on either side.
Activity peaks near an optimum pH (~7.5) and falls off away from it.

Reactor & airflow

Airflow controls how quickly air passes through the reactor. Higher flow generally means less contact time.
Greater active biomass can increase modeled photosynthetic capacity, up to a saturation limit.

Result

Simulation assumptions

  • Algae require light as an energy source for photosynthesis.
  • Photosynthesis is modeled as the primary CO₂-consuming process.
  • Growth depends on light, CO₂ availability, temperature, pH and nutrients, combined as simplified multiplicative factors.
  • Gas–liquid CO₂ transfer between air and the algae culture is simplified, not solved from first principles.
  • Actual CO₂ removal in a real reactor depends heavily on physical design, mixing, and bubble contact area.
  • Real-world performance requires experimental testing and cannot be inferred from this model alone.
  • Algae systems modeled here do not automatically remove particulates, VOCs, NOx, bacteria, or viruses.

Limitations of this simulator

  • Simplified biological model — not species-specific.
  • Simplified gas transfer between air and liquid phases.
  • No detailed algae species or strain-level model.
  • No real sensor calibration — sensor values are simulated.
  • No experimental validation against a physical reactor.
  • Does not guarantee real indoor air purification performance.
  • O₂ values are estimates, not measurements.
  • Energy consumption figures are estimates based on user-entered power ratings.
  • Actual reactor performance may differ significantly from simulated values.
Educational simulation — all values are model assumptions and must be experimentally validated before drawing real-world conclusions.

Mathematical model summary

P = P_max · f(B) · f(L) · f(T) · f(pH) · f(CO₂)
C_out = max(C_min, C_in − removal(P, residence time))
dB/dt = growth_rate(P) · B − decay_rate · B
O₂ production ≈ P · oxygen_conversion_factor
